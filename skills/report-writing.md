# Skill: Engineering Report Writing

## Purpose

This skill covers the preparation of structural engineering reports — including structural assessments, feasibility studies, forensic investigations, condition surveys, and construction observation reports. Reports are the primary deliverable for non-design work and carry the same professional weight as calculation packages.

## Report Types

### Structural Assessment Report
- Evaluates existing structure for proposed modification, change of use, or damage
- Scope: visual observation, limited analysis, recommendations
- Audience: building owner, architect, building official

### Feasibility Study
- Evaluates structural viability of proposed concept
- Scope: preliminary analysis, system selection, cost/schedule implications
- Audience: owner, architect, developer

### Forensic Investigation Report
- Documents structural failure, distress, or deficiency
- Scope: observations (objective), analysis, opinions (with basis), recommendations
- Must maintain clear separation between facts and opinions
- May be used in litigation — write accordingly

### Condition Survey / Due Diligence
- Documents existing structural condition for real estate transaction or maintenance planning
- Scope: visual observation, document review, rating/classification
- Audience: buyer, lender, property manager

### Construction Observation Report
- Documents structural observations during construction
- Scope: specific items observed, conformance with plans, deficiencies noted
- Audience: building official, owner, contractor, project file

## Report Structure — Standard Format

```
RABUILT ENGINEERING
[Report Type]

PROJECT: [Name]
PROJECT NUMBER: [###]
DATE: [Date]
PREPARED BY: Ray Tennal, PE
WA PE LICENSE #: [Number]

TABLE OF CONTENTS

1.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
    [1-2 paragraph summary of findings and recommendations]

2.0 INTRODUCTION
    2.1 Purpose and Scope
    2.2 Authorization
    2.3 Limitations and Exclusions

3.0 BACKGROUND
    3.1 Building Description
    3.2 Construction History
    3.3 Documents Reviewed
    3.4 Applicable Codes and Standards

4.0 OBSERVATIONS
    4.1 [Area/Element 1]
    4.2 [Area/Element 2]
    [Objective descriptions with photo references]

5.0 ANALYSIS
    [If applicable — calculations, code checks, capacity evaluations]

6.0 FINDINGS AND OPINIONS
    [Clearly stated professional opinions with basis]

7.0 RECOMMENDATIONS
    7.1 Immediate Actions (if any)
    7.2 Short-Term Recommendations
    7.3 Long-Term Recommendations
    [Prioritized, actionable, with rough scope indications]

8.0 LIMITATIONS
    [Standard limitations of visual observation, scope boundaries]

APPENDICES
    A. Photographs
    B. Drawings / Sketches
    C. Calculations (if applicable)
    D. Reference Documents
```

## Writing Standards

### General
- **Active voice preferred.** "The beam shows signs of corrosion" not "Signs of corrosion were observed on the beam."
- **Be specific.** "The 4x12 ridge beam at Grid B has a visible crack approximately 1/4-inch wide extending 24 inches from the east support" — not "a beam is cracked."
- **Quantify.** Dimensions, quantities, locations, measurements. "Approximately" is fine; vagueness is not.
- **Reference locations consistently.** Use grid lines, compass directions, floor levels, or photo numbers to locate observations.
- **Separate observation from opinion.** Observations are facts; opinions are professional judgments. The reader must be able to distinguish them.

### Observation Descriptions
For each observation, include:
1. Location (specific — grid, floor, compass direction)
2. Element (structural member type, size, material)
3. Condition observed (specific, measurable where possible)
4. Photograph reference
5. Significance (does this affect structural capacity, safety, serviceability?)

### Opinion Statements
Must include:
1. The opinion itself (clear, direct)
2. The basis for the opinion (observations, analysis, code reference, experience)
3. The confidence level (where appropriate)
4. Qualifications or caveats

**Example:**
> "In my professional opinion, the existing roof framing is adequate to support the proposed solar panel array, based on the analysis presented in Appendix C. This opinion assumes the existing roof framing is Douglas Fir-Larch No. 2 or better, which is consistent with the era of construction but was not confirmed by destructive testing."

### Limitations Section — Standard Language

Include limitations appropriate to scope:

> "This report is based on visual observation of accessible and visible structural elements on the date(s) of observation noted herein. Concealed conditions were not evaluated. No destructive or invasive testing was performed unless specifically noted. This report does not constitute a guarantee or warranty regarding the structural condition of the building."

> "This report has been prepared for the exclusive use of [Client Name] for the specific purpose described herein. Use by other parties or for other purposes is not authorized without written consent of RaBuilt Engineering."

## Specific Report Guidelines

### Forensic Reports
- Maintain objectivity — you may be retained by one party but your opinions serve the truth
- Document chain of custody for physical evidence
- Photograph everything before disturbing
- Date and time all observations
- Consider whether destructive testing is warranted
- If cause of failure is clear, state it directly with basis
- If cause is uncertain, present possibilities ranked by likelihood

### Construction Observation Reports
- Date, time, weather conditions
- Specific items observed (per scope of services)
- Conformance statement: "The observed work appears to be in general conformance with the contract documents" or note specific deficiencies
- Items to be resolved before proceeding
- Next anticipated observation visit

### Structural Assessment for Modification
- Document existing structure thoroughly
- Identify the proposed modification clearly
- Analyze existing structure for new loads/conditions
- Identify elements that are adequate as-is
- Identify elements requiring reinforcement
- Provide conceptual reinforcement schemes with scope indication
- Reference applicable code provisions for existing buildings (IEBC)

## Photograph Standards

- Include date stamp or note in caption
- Identify location and direction
- Include scale reference where relevant (tape measure, known object)
- Number photographs consecutively
- Reference photo numbers in report text
- Minimum resolution: sufficient for enlarged viewing of details

## Common Errors to Avoid

- Mixing observations and opinions without clear delineation
- Being vague about locations ("a wall" vs. "the east bearing wall at Grid 3")
- Overstepping scope (opining on items not within structural engineering)
- Providing cost estimates in engineering reports (qualify heavily or avoid)
- Not including appropriate limitations language
- Forgetting to state assumptions underlying opinions
- Using technical jargon without definition for non-engineer audiences
- Not referencing photographs in the body of the report

## Deliverable Checklist

- [ ] Report follows standard format structure
- [ ] Executive summary captures key findings and recommendations
- [ ] All observations are specific and located
- [ ] Opinions are separated from observations with stated basis
- [ ] Photographs are referenced and captioned
- [ ] Limitations section is included and appropriate
- [ ] Codes and standards referenced where applicable
- [ ] Report reviewed for grammar, consistency, and accuracy
- [ ] PE stamp and signature (final version)
